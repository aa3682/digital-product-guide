# About this guide

Written and maintained by one person who is building and selling a first digital product on the side. Independent, unsponsored, and open: prose is CC BY 4.0, code is MIT.

## What this is

This is a free, public reference on launching and selling a digital product on your own. It is written first for working professionals building a first digital product on the side, and second for creators who already sell and want the business side tightened up, so every page starts from plain English and marks where it speaks to someone who already sells.

The site is organized by The Launch Path at the top level, in the order the work actually follows. Beneath that sits The Business Side, the areas of rules and obligations the steps draw on, and a set of tools that make the work concrete.

## What it is not

This guide is general education for people building and selling a digital product on their own. It is not legal, tax, accounting, or business advice for your situation, and reading it does not create a professional relationship.

## How figures are handled

The body pages on this site never print a limit, threshold, rate, deadline, or fee set by law, regulation, an agency, or a platform. Those numbers change, and a figure printed in the middle of a paragraph goes stale without anyone noticing. Instead, a page names the concept in plain words and links to the one page that holds the number.

That page is [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures). Every figure there sits in its own row with the year it applies to and a link to the primary source it was taken from. The page is checked against its sources once a year. It is also the only page on the site that names a platform, and only as the source of that platform's own fee.

## How sources are chosen

When a page needs support for a claim, it cites a public primary source rather than a secondary summary: federal and state statutes and regulations, IRS publications and form instructions, state revenue agency pages, FTC guidance, Copyright Office circulars, USPTO pages, and, for a platform's own fees, that platform's published fee or terms page. Commentary, marketing material, and other people's guides are not used as sources. Where a page rests on judgment rather than a source, it says so.

## Who writes it

All of the writing is original, and no page reproduces text from any book, course, blog post, or platform help page.

## Corrections

If you find an error, a broken link, or a figure that is out of date, report it on the repository's [Issues page](https://github.com/aa3682/digital-product-guide/issues). Say which page and which line, and if you can, link the source that shows the correct fact.
