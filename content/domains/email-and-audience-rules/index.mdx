# Email and Audience Rules

This area covers the rules for building and using a list: what counts as a commercial message, what every such message must carry, how opting out works, what is different about text messages, and what changes when a subscriber is outside the United States. The law sets a floor; the tools you send with usually set a higher bar.

## Key ideas

### What the CAN-SPAM Act covers

The CAN-SPAM Act applies to any [commercial message](/glossary#commercial-message): an email whose primary purpose is to advertise or promote a product or service. One message to a former buyer counts, and so does every email in a launch sequence. The law has no exception for small senders and none for business-to-business mail; a company address is covered like a personal one.

A [transactional message](/glossary#transactional-message) is different. A receipt, a download link, a password reset, or a notice about an account the recipient already has is outside most of the Act's requirements. It still may not carry a misleading header or subject line. An email that mixes the two is judged by its primary purpose. U.S. law does not require consent before a first commercial email. It is an opt-out regime: the recipient may be written to until they say stop, and the stop must be honored.

### What every commercial email must carry

Six things. The header information, meaning the from, to, and reply-to lines and the routing behind them, must be accurate and identify the sender. The subject line may not mislead about the content. The message must be identifiable as an advertisement, though the law leaves room for how, and one the recipient plainly signed up for makes that obvious. The message must carry a valid physical postal address; a post office box or a box at a registered commercial mail receiving agency counts. It must give the recipient a working way to [opt out](/glossary#opt-out), and that request must be honored within the window the law sets, which is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#email-and-audience-rules).

Opting out cannot cost the recipient anything and cannot demand more than a reply or a visit to a single page. It cannot require a login or a reason. Once an address has opted out, it cannot be sold, rented, or passed to anyone else, except to a contractor sending on your behalf so they can honor the request. A member or subscriber may opt out of your marketing email and keep the membership. The messages the membership itself requires, such as a renewal notice or a change to access, are [transactional messages](/glossary#transactional-message) and continue.

### Consent as the tools require it

The law's opt-out model is the floor. The email services sellers use almost always require, in their own terms, that everyone on a list agreed to be there. They forbid purchased or scraped lists, and they suspend accounts when complaint rates climb, whether or not the law was broken.

Whether a person agreed depends on what was said where the address was collected. A person who gives an address for a free download has agreed to receive that download. Whether they have also agreed to ongoing marketing depends on whether the form said so. What the shop then does with the address belongs in the privacy notice, which [Terms, Refunds, and Privacy](/domains/terms-refunds-and-privacy) covers.

### Texts

Marketing text messages fall under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act and the FCC's rules under it, not the CAN-SPAM Act. An automated marketing text requires prior express written consent: a signed or electronically signed agreement, made before the first text, that names the sender and says the person agrees to receive marketing texts at that number. A stop request must be honored. Many states add their own text rules on top. The statute sets damages per message, which is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#email-and-audience-rules).

### Subscribers outside the United States

Many countries, including the closest trading partners, take the opposite approach to email. They require opt-in consent before a commercial message is sent rather than a way to opt out afterward, and they apply their law based on where the recipient is, not where the sender is. A list with subscribers in those countries is subject to those rules for those subscribers, and the record of how each consented is what a sender would rely on if asked.

### Whose responsibility it is

The seller whose product is promoted is responsible for the message, whoever sends it. An email service, a contractor, or an affiliate sending on your behalf does not move the obligation. The same holds for the list itself: a [suppression list](/glossary#suppression-list), the record of every address that has opted out, has to be one list, shared across every tool that sends. An address that opts out in one tool and is still mailed from another has not been honored.

## Where it shows up in the process

- [Set Up the Shop](/process/set-up-the-shop): The signup form and its wording decide what each subscriber has agreed to.
- [Launch](/process/launch): The launch sequence is a series of commercial messages, each carrying the required footer and opt-out.
- [Keep Selling](/process/keep-selling): The list is kept, opt-outs are honored, and the suppression list is shared across tools for as long as anything is sent.

## Common misconceptions

- That a small list is exempt. The Act has no volume threshold; one commercial email is covered.
- That business addresses are exempt. The Act treats a company address the same as a personal one.
- That a free-download signup is consent to everything afterward. It is consent to the download; the form's wording decides the rest.
- That using an email service shifts the legal responsibility. The seller remains responsible for messages sent on its behalf.
- That "reply STOP" alone satisfies the email opt-out rule. It is the text-message convention; an email needs a working opt-out the recipient can use by reply or one page, and the postal address.

## For practitioners

<Callout type="info">
This area leaves three things running. Opt-outs are honored within the window in every tool that sends, not only the main one. The postal address in the footer is kept current as the business moves. And consent records are kept for subscribers outside the United States, since their law may ask how each of them joined. If you have been sending without these, check first whether every tool that sends shares one suppression list, and whether the address in the footer is still the right one.
</Callout>
