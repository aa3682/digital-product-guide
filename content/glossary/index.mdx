# Glossary

This page defines every term the guide links to, in plain English and for education rather than advice. Any number a term depends on, such as a limit, rate, or age, lives on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures) rather than here.

### access product [#access-product]

A digital product the buyer enters rather than downloads, such as a course or a membership. You keep the space running after the sale, so the buyer's access depends on you; a [membership](#membership) is the ongoing kind.

### anchor [#anchor]

In pricing, the comparison a buyer makes when they first see your price: what they would otherwise spend to solve the same problem, whether an afternoon of their own time, a hire, or a larger product. Your price reads as fair or not against that comparison.

### audience [#audience]

The people who have agreed to hear from you, usually by giving you their email address on purpose. It is the group you may write to about a launch; everyone else is left alone.

### cadence [#cadence]

A fixed rhythm for a recurring task, chosen once so the task happens without a fresh decision each time. The guide uses it for updating your ledger and for reviewing and revising a product.

### chargeback [#chargeback]

A reversal of a card payment that the buyer requests from their card issuer rather than from you. The amount comes back out of your account, your payment processor usually adds a fee, and you have a limited window to answer with evidence. The current figure is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#payments-and-chargebacks).

### clear and conspicuous [#clear-and-conspicuous]

The standard a disclosure must meet: hard to miss and easy to understand for an ordinary member of the audience. In practice it sits next to the claim it qualifies, in the same medium, in plain words, with no click or scroll needed to find it.

### commercial message [#commercial-message]

An email whose primary purpose is to advertise or promote a product or service. A launch announcement is one, and so is a single note to a past buyer about something new for sale; the email rules apply to every one of them.

### copyright [#copyright]

The set of exclusive rights the law gives the author of an original work, including the rights to copy, distribute, adapt, display, and perform it. It exists as soon as the work is saved in a form that can be read back, with no notice and no registration required.

### deliverable [#deliverable]

The thing that actually changes hands when someone buys. For a downloadable product it is the file itself; for a course or membership it is the first complete set of material plus a written schedule for what follows.

### digital product [#digital-product]

A product sold as a file the buyer downloads or as an online space the buyer pays to enter. Ebooks, workbooks, templates, courses, and memberships are the kinds this guide covers.

### endorsement [#endorsement]

Any message a buyer would take as the opinion of someone other than the seller, such as a review, a testimonial, or a recommendation from an affiliate. The seller is responsible for what its endorsers say and for whether they disclose their connection to it.

### entity [#entity]

A legal form the state recognizes as separate from its owner, created by filing with the state. For a one-person business the common one is a limited liability company; until you form one, you are a [sole proprietor](#sole-proprietor).

### estimated tax [#estimated-tax]

Federal tax paid in installments through the year on set due dates, because no one withholds tax from a sale. Payments are expected once the tax you will owe for the year passes a threshold, and missing a due date can add a penalty even if the return is paid in full. The current figure is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#taxes-and-records).

### fair use [#fair-use]

A defense that allows some quotation and reuse of copyrighted material without permission. It is decided case by case, weighing several factors, and no fixed percentage or word count makes a use fair.

### format [#format]

The shape a product takes: an ebook, a workbook, a template, a course, or a membership. The format decides what you build, what the buyer receives, and what you owe them after the sale.

### launch discount [#launch-discount]

A lower price offered for a stated period around a launch. It needs a reason you can say plainly and an end date you keep, because a deadline that quietly extends is a false claim.

### license [#license]

The permission that says what someone may do with a copyrighted work, and on what conditions. You sell your product under one, which tells the buyer whether they may share or resell it, and you buy stock images, fonts, and templates under one, which tells you whether they may go into a product for sale.

### list price [#list-price]

The price shown on the sales page. It is not what reaches you, because fees and any sales tax collected come out before you see your [net](#net).

### marketplace [#marketplace]

A site that already has buyers browsing it and sells your product to them in its own name. It brings the buyers, keeps a larger share of each sale, and holds the customer relationship, so the buyer is its customer rather than yours.

### marketplace facilitator [#marketplace-facilitator]

A [marketplace](#marketplace) that state law makes responsible for collecting and remitting sales tax on the sales made through it, so the sellers using it do not. Most states have such a law, and each state decides which marketplaces it covers. The current figure is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#taxes-and-records).

### material connection [#material-connection]

A relationship between a seller and an endorser that the audience would not expect and that could affect how they weigh the [endorsement](#endorsement). An affiliate commission, a free copy, a payment, a job, or a close personal tie is one, and it must be disclosed.

### membership [#membership]

An access product the buyer pays for on a schedule, in return for which you owe them something new each period. Because the charges continue until the buyer stops them, it is also a [negative option](#negative-option).

### merchant of record [#merchant-of-record]

The business named on the buyer's card statement and legally making the sale. On your own storefront with a payment processor that is you; on a marketplace it is the marketplace; on a hosted storefront, the service's terms say which.

### negative option [#negative-option]

An offer where the buyer's silence counts as yes, so charges continue until the buyer acts to stop them; a membership billed each period is one, and so is a free trial that converts to paid. Federal and state rules set what must be disclosed before billing and how simple cancelling must be. The current figure is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#terms-refunds-and-privacy).

### net [#net]

What is left of a sale after the storefront or payment processor takes its cut and any sales tax collected from the buyer is set aside. It is the figure to plan around and the one your records carry.

### nexus [#nexus]

The connection between a seller and a state that lets the state require the seller to collect its sales tax. A physical presence creates it, and so can sales into the state above a level the state sets, with no presence there at all. The current figure is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#taxes-and-records).

### opt-out [#opt-out]

A recipient's request to stop receiving your commercial email, and the working way every such email must give them to make it. The request cannot cost them anything or require a login, and it must be honored within the window the law sets. The current figure is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#email-and-audience-rules).

### payment processor [#payment-processor]

The service that takes the buyer's card and moves the money to you, keeping a fee from each transaction. A storefront usually has one built in, and its published terms set the fees, the payout schedule, and the rules for disputes.

### payout [#payout]

The transfer of what you are owed from the [payment processor](#payment-processor) to your bank account. It arrives on the processor's schedule, may carry a transfer fee, and can be held or delayed when the processor sees risk. The current figure is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#payments-and-chargebacks).

### privacy notice [#privacy-notice]

The page that tells buyers what personal information your shop collects, why, which services receive it, how long it is kept, and how a buyer can ask about it. It describes what your shop actually does, so it changes when your tools do.

### public domain [#public-domain]

Material in which copyright has expired or never applied, so anyone may use it without permission. That a work is free to view online does not put it in the public domain.

### refund policy [#refund-policy]

Your stated rule on when a buyer can get their money back. It must be shown before purchase and honored as written, and it works alongside state consumer-protection law and the rules of the storefront or marketplace you sell through.

### reserve [#reserve]

A share of each sale that your payment processor holds back for a period before releasing it to you. A new account, a sudden jump in volume, or a run of disputes can trigger one, and the processor's terms say how much and for how long. The current figure is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#payments-and-chargebacks).

### retire [#retire]

To take a product off sale on purpose. Buyers keep what they bought and can still reach it, and the sales page says the product is no longer sold rather than disappearing.

### sole proprietor [#sole-proprietor]

One person running a business with no legal line between the person and the business. It is what you are by default from the first sale, with nothing filed, until you form an [entity](#entity).

### storefront [#storefront]

A shop of your own, on a store service or course platform, where you bring the buyers, keep more of each sale, and hold the list of who bought. Which of you is the [merchant of record](#merchant-of-record) depends on whether you or the service processes the payment.

### substantiation [#substantiation]

The evidence you hold, before a claim is made, that is adequate to what the claim says. A claim you cannot back up is one you cannot make, however true it feels.

### suppression list [#suppression-list]

The record of every address that has sent you an [opt-out](#opt-out). It has to be one list, shared across every tool that sends on your behalf, because an address that opts out in one tool and is still mailed from another has not been honored.

### terms of sale [#terms-of-sale]

The agreement between you and the buyer that governs the sale: what they are getting, what they may do with it, and what happens if something goes wrong. Shown before payment and accepted with a click, they form a contract; shown only afterward, they are weak.

### transactional message [#transactional-message]

An email the recipient's purchase or account itself requires, such as a receipt, a download link, a password reset, or a renewal notice. It is outside most of the rules for a [commercial message](#commercial-message), though its header and subject line still may not mislead.

### version [#version]

A numbered release of your product. Each new version gets a number and a short note of what changed, so a buyer can tell which one they have and whether an update is due to them.

### work made for hire [#work-made-for-hire]

A work whose copyright belongs from the start to the person who commissioned it rather than to the person who made it. For a contractor's work it is a narrow category that requires a signed written agreement, so hired work usually needs a written assignment instead.
