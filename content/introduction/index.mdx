# Introduction

This guide explains how to launch and sell a digital product on your own, from choosing what to make to keeping it selling after launch day. It is free, public, and written in plain English.

## What this guide is

This is an open reference on launching and selling a digital product on your own. It covers downloadable and access products: ebooks, workbooks, templates, courses, and memberships. It does not cover software, apps, subscription software, or physical goods, because those bring their own delivery, support, and legal questions that this guide does not try to answer.

## Who it is for

The first audience is working professionals building a first digital product on the side, with limited hours and no background in running a business. The second audience is creators who already sell and want the business side tightened up. Every page is written for the first reader. Where a page speaks to the second, it does so in a clearly marked callout, so both readers follow the same pages.

## One assumption

This guide assumes U.S. law and U.S. tax rules. State rules differ on several points, and the guide says so where it matters rather than pretending there is one answer. If you are outside the United States, treat [The Launch Path](/process) as portable and treat the legal and tax pages in [The Business Side](/domains) as a checklist of questions to ask locally. This is the only place on the site the assumption is explained.

## How it is organized

- **[The Launch Path](/process).** Six steps, in order, from choosing the product to keeping it selling.
- **[The Business Side](/domains).** Seven areas of rules and ongoing obligations that cut across the steps.
- **[Tools](/tools).** Calculators, checklists, and worksheets that support specific steps.
- **[Glossary](/glossary).** Plain definitions of the terms used throughout the guide.

## How to use it

If you are building your first product, read [The Launch Path](/process) in order. Each step produces what the next one needs. If you already sell, go straight to [The Business Side](/domains). Every area page stands on its own, and its callout tells you what to check first.
