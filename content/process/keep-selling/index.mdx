# Keep Selling

This step is the long tail. The launch week is over, and the product now has to sell to people who were not there for it, while you carry the obligations each sale created and gather what the next product will be built from.

## Why it matters

Most of a digital product's sales come after the launch, one at a time, to people who found it on the day they had the problem. A product sold only on launch day earns a week of income for weeks of work. The next product depends on this step too: what sold, what was asked, and what was refunded are its demand check, and they only exist if you kept them.

Skip it and the shop goes quiet in one of two ways. The page drifts out of date, the file stops matching the description, and buyers who arrive stop trusting it. Or the obligations lapse: a filing date passes, a chargeback goes unanswered, a membership period arrives with nothing new in it.

## What actually happens

### Stay findable

The product sells when someone meets the promise at the moment they have the problem, so it has to stay findable. The page stays up, at the same link, so a recommendation made months ago still works. The description uses the words buyers search, which you learned at the promise test and in the first week's questions. And free answers lead to it: a short public answer to a question you keep getting, with a link to the product for the full version. Priya writes one page answering the state-form question three buyers asked, and it becomes where new supervisors find the checklist.

### Keep the list

The list is buyers and the people who agreed to hear from you. If you chose a storefront, it is the one asset the launch created that nobody else holds. Write to it when something is new: a version, an answer, a second product. Not on a schedule with nothing to say. Every message goes only to people who agreed, and every one carries a way to stop; the rules that govern that are in [Email and Audience Rules](/domains/email-and-audience-rules).

### Update on a cadence

A [cadence](/glossary#cadence) is a fixed rhythm for doing something, chosen so it happens without a decision each time. Set one for the product: a look at the questions and the file, and a new version when they call for it. Each new version gets a number, a short note of what changed, and word to past buyers where the price included updates. Priya reviews the checklist twice a year, when the forms it covers tend to change, and sends the new version to every buyer from the past year.

### Carry the obligations

Selling created obligations that last as long as the page is up. Refunds are honored within the window you promised, and a chargeback is answered by its deadline with the records you kept; [Payments and Chargebacks](/domains/payments-and-chargebacks) covers both. Records are kept and filings made on their dates, which [Taxes and Records](/domains/taxes-and-records) sets out. A membership's promise is kept every period. The terms are kept current with the product, so what the page says the buyer gets is what the file delivers. And once a year, check [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures) against your setup: the storefront's cut, the sales tax setting, the filing thresholds, and the rules for the list.

### Read the records and choose the next product

The records now answer the question the first step asked from guesses. What sold, and to whom. What buyers asked, in their words. What was refunded, and why. That list is the demand check for the next product, already done; Priya's shows the state-form question, asked by a third of her buyers, is what people would pay for next. The path loops back to Choose the Product with that list in hand.

Sometimes the reading says to stop. To [retire](/glossary#retire) a product is to take it off sale on purpose. Do it honestly: buyers keep what they bought and can still reach it, the page says the product is no longer sold rather than vanishing, and a membership gets notice before its last period.

## Questions to ask yourself

- If someone recommended the product six months ago, does the link still work and match the file?
- When did you last write to the list, and did you have something new to say?
- Which obligations have dates, and where are those dates written down?
- What do the questions and refunds since launch say about the next product?

## Common mistakes

- Treating the launch as the sale; most buyers arrive later and need a page that still works.
- Writing to the list on a schedule with nothing to say; each empty message costs a few readers.
- Updating the file without telling past buyers; the ones whose price included updates notice.
- Letting the terms describe an old version; the promise on the page drifts from the product.
- Retiring by deleting; buyers lose what they paid for, and the refund requests follow.

## Which business areas apply

- [Business Setup](/domains/business-setup): An annual report for the entity, a license renewal, and a disclosure to an employer come around on their own schedule while the product sells.
- [Taxes and Records](/domains/taxes-and-records): Records and filings run on dates that arrive whether or not anything sold.
- [Copyright and Licensing](/domains/copyright-and-licensing): Every update adds an entry to the rights list, and this is when copies of your product start to turn up elsewhere.
- [Terms, Refunds, and Privacy](/domains/terms-refunds-and-privacy): The terms must keep describing the product as it is now, and a retirement changes what they promise.
- [Payments and Chargebacks](/domains/payments-and-chargebacks): Refunds and chargebacks continue as long as the page is up, and each has a deadline.
- [Email and Audience Rules](/domains/email-and-audience-rules): Every message to the list, for as long as you keep it, follows the same consent and opt-out rules as the launch.
- [Marketing Claims and Disclosures](/domains/marketing-claims-and-disclosures): Testimonials, reviews, and affiliates show up once the product has buyers, and each one carries a disclosure you must make.

## For practitioners

<Callout type="info">
A catalog is only as trustworthy as its least maintained page. Check every product's page against its current file, and retire what you no longer maintain rather than leaving it on sale. If buyers are spread across several lists, consolidate them into one you control, keeping only the people who agreed to hear from you. Put the yearly checks on a date: the terms against each product, the filings, and the figures page against your setup. A cadence written down survives a busy quarter; one you meant to keep does not.
</Callout>
