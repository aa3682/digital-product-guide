# Set Up the Shop

This step is the setup. You choose where the product is sold, arrange how a buyer receives it, connect the money, write the pages a buyer reads, and give them a way to reach you. It ends when you have bought it yourself.

## Why it matters

The launch sends people to a link. Everything behind that link is built here: the page they read, the file or space they get, and the person who answers when something goes wrong. A launch on a half-built shop is spent on support.

Skip the care, and the cost arrives with the first buyers: a buyer pays and the file does not come, or payouts land in a personal account and the first tax season becomes a reconstruction.

## What actually happens

### Choose where it sells

There are two kinds of place to sell. A [marketplace](/glossary#marketplace) is a site that already has buyers browsing it. It brings them to you, keeps a larger share of each sale, and holds the relationship: the buyer is its customer, and their email stays with it. A [storefront](/glossary#storefront) is a shop of your own, on a store service or course platform. You bring the buyers, keep more, and hold the list of who bought.

For a first product, pick the one you can set up this week. The first launch is for proof, which does not depend on the venue. What each kind keeps from a sale changes, so it lives on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#payments-and-chargebacks). Priya chooses a storefront, because the supervisors she wants to reach are people she can already email.

### Set up delivery

For a file, upload it and name the file with its version, so a support question can be matched to what the buyer holds. Then check what the buyer receives and when: a link on the confirmation page, an email, or both, and how long it works. For a course or membership, the questions are how a buyer gets in and what happens to access when a membership ends. Decide that now, because it belongs in the terms. Priya's upload is one file, named with its version number, sent on the confirmation page and again by email.

### Set up the money

Three settings, each deciding something later. The [payment processor](/glossary#payment-processor) is the service that takes the buyer's card and moves the money; a storefront usually has one built in. Open the account in the right name, yours or the business's, per [Business Setup](/domains/business-setup). A [payout](/glossary#payout) is the transfer of what you are owed from the processor to your bank. Send it to an account kept apart from personal money, because your records start with the first one; [Taxes and Records](/domains/taxes-and-records) explains what they must show. Then the sales tax setting: whether the storefront collects tax from the buyer and passes it on, or leaves that to you. The concepts behind that choice are on [the taxes section of this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#taxes-and-records).

### Write the pages a buyer reads

A buyer reads three things. The description, which must match the product; the one written in Make the Product is the draft. The terms, which carry the license sentence from Make the Product, the refund promise from Set the Price, and what the price includes. And a privacy notice, because from the first sale you hold names, email addresses, and payment records.

The storefront may supply templates for both. Use them, and read them, because the promises in them are yours once they are on your page; what each needs is in [Terms, Refunds, and Privacy](/domains/terms-refunds-and-privacy). Priya's terms are the template with her license sentence, her two-week refund promise, and a line saying the price includes a year of updates.

### Set up support and buy it yourself

Support for a first product is small. One email address you check, a reply window you can keep alongside a job, and a short page answering the questions test readers asked, because buyers ask the same ones. Make sure you can issue a refund in minutes, from your phone. A fast refund is the cheapest outcome with an unhappy buyer; a slow one becomes a chargeback, which [Payments and Chargebacks](/domains/payments-and-chargebacks) explains costs more.

Then buy the product on a phone, with a real card, open the file or enter the space, and refund yourself. Where you stalled, a buyer will stall. Priya's test purchase opens a preview her phone cannot read, and she fixes the file type before a buyer sees it.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Which venue can you have running this week, and what does it keep from each sale?
- What does a buyer receive, by what route, and how soon after paying?
- Where do payouts land, and is that account separate from personal money?
- Have you bought the product yourself, on a phone, and received it?

## Common mistakes

- Comparing venues for weeks; the first product needs a shop, not the best one.
- Payouts into a personal account; every later record must be pulled apart from groceries.
- Pasting template terms without reading them; the refund promise on the page is the one you owe.
- Skipping the test purchase; the first buyer becomes the test and reports the result in public.

## Which business areas apply

- [Business Setup](/domains/business-setup): The name on the processor and storefront accounts is the entity decision applied.
- [Terms, Refunds, and Privacy](/domains/terms-refunds-and-privacy): The terms, the refund window, and the privacy notice are published here.
- [Payments and Chargebacks](/domains/payments-and-chargebacks): The processor's cut, the refund mechanics, and what a chargeback costs follow from the account you open.
- [Taxes and Records](/domains/taxes-and-records): The payout account and the sales tax setting are where your records begin.

## For practitioners

<Callout type="info">
Buy each of your products today, on a phone, and follow the delivery through; a link that worked at launch may point at a version two updates old. Read the terms against the current product and your current refund practice, because the page tends to describe the product and promise you launched with. Check that payouts still land in a separate account and the sales tax setting matches where you sell now. If a marketplace holds your buyers' emails, start a list you control now.
</Callout>
