# Pricing Worksheet

This worksheet belongs to [Set the Price](/process/set-the-price), the third step of The Launch Path. It gathers what the product cost you, what the buyer would do without it, and what comes out of each sale before anything reaches you, and it ends with one price and a written reason. Fill it in after the product exists, because the price is set against what the file does.

## How to use it

Print it or copy the questions into your own notes and answer them with your own figures. Every number you write is your own: hours, costs, the price, the count of sales. Any number set by law, an agency, or a platform lives on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures), and this page links to it where one comes up rather than printing it.

## Costs

The cost of making a digital product does not set its price; the buyer never sees your hours. Write costs down anyway, because they answer a different question later: how many sales pay them back.

1. How many hours went into the build, and what would you pay yourself for an hour of them?
2. What did you spend on software, images, or help from other people?
3. What will it cost each month to keep the product for sale: hosting, an email service, a fixed storefront fee if there is one?
4. Add the first two into one figure: what the product cost to make.

Example: Priya's build took three weeks of evenings and one paid tool, and she writes both down as her cost to make.

## Price

The [anchor](/glossary#anchor) is what the buyer compares the price to: what they would do without the product.

1. What would the buyer do without this: spend an afternoon on it, hire someone, buy a bigger course, or get it wrong and fix it later?
2. What does that alternative cost them in time or money?
3. What round number sits well below that alternative and clearly above free?
4. Can you say the number without a pause? If not, round it again.
5. Write the reason under the price in one sentence.
6. What does the price include: updates, questions, and for a membership, the period and what canceling means?

Example: Priya's note reads "Forty dollars. Below a morning of a supervisor's time, above what a free search would make anyone trust."

## What the platform keeps

The [list price](/glossary#list-price) is not what reaches you. The [storefront](/glossary#storefront) or [payment processor](/glossary#payment-processor) keeps a cut, and sales tax collected from the buyer passes through you to the state. What is left is your [net](/glossary#net), the number to plan around. The cuts are set by each platform's own terms, and the rows for them are on [this year's figures under Payments and Chargebacks](/tools/this-years-figures#payments-and-chargebacks).

1. Which venue will you sell on, and what does its published fee page say it keeps from one sale?
2. Is there a separate processing fee on top, and a fee on payouts?
3. Does the venue collect sales tax for you, or is that yours, where the buyer's state taxes digital products?
4. Subtract the cuts from the list price: what is your net from one sale?

Example: Priya sells a forty-dollar checklist, the storefront and processor keep a few dollars between them, and her net is what she plans around.

## Break-even count

The break-even count is how many sales pay back what the product cost to make. It does not set the price; it tells you what the first months have to do.

1. Divide the cost to make by the net from one sale. How many sales pay it back?
2. Add the monthly running cost. How many sales a month keep the product above water?
3. Is that count realistic for the audience you listed in the product choice worksheet?
4. If not, is the fix a higher price, a lower cost, or a wider audience?

Example: If Priya's net is thirty dollars and her cost to make was six hundred, twenty sales pay the build back.

## Discount rules

A [launch discount](/glossary#launch-discount) is a decision for the [Launch](/process/launch) step, not part of the price. Set the rules now so the launch does not set them for you.

1. Will there be a launch discount at all, and can you say its reason in one sentence?
2. When does it end, and will you hold that date?
3. Will you ever show a former price beside the current one? If so, the product must actually have sold at it.
4. What happens to past buyers when the price changes: do they keep their price, and for how long?

Example: Priya offers no discount, because she has no reason for one.

## Business Side

- [Taxes and Records](/domains/taxes-and-records): Sales tax collected from a buyer is never yours, and the net from each sale, after the storefront's cut and sales tax, is what you plan around and report; whether a state taxes digital products is pointed to on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#taxes-and-records).
- [Terms, Refunds, and Privacy](/domains/terms-refunds-and-privacy): What the price covers and the refund promise are set here and become terms the buyer accepts.
- [Payments and Chargebacks](/domains/payments-and-chargebacks): The share a storefront or payment processor takes from each sale sets what a given list price leaves you, and each cut is on [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#payments-and-chargebacks).
- [Marketing Claims and Disclosures](/domains/marketing-claims-and-disclosures): A former price shown beside the current one, and any guarantee attached to the price, are claims you must be able to back.
